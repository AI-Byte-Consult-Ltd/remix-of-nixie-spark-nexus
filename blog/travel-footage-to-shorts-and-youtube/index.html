One Trip, Two Videos: How to Turn Travel Footage into Shorts, Reels and a YouTube Video

Published: 2026-10-29 · AI Byte Consult · Read in: Русский

A step-by-step walkthrough on a real Hawaii trip — how 30 raw phone clips become a vertical video for Shorts and Reels, a horizontal YouTube video and covers in two languages.

After a trip, your phone holds dozens of short clips: the beach, the road, the food, the sunset, another sunset. Editing even one video out of them takes an evening, sometimes a weekend. That is why most holiday footage never leaves the camera roll.

Here is how we turn one folder of clips into two finished videos at once: a vertical one for Shorts, Reels and TikTok, and a horizontal one for YouTube. The example is real: a trip to Hawaii, 30 clips shot on a phone.

Why the same footage needs two different videos

A vertical short and a horizontal YouTube video are not the same edit in two sizes. They do different jobs.

Shorts / Reels / TikTokYouTube
Frame9:16, vertical16:9, horizontal
Lengthabout 30 seconds1–1.5 minutes
Paceshort cuts, fast transitionslonger shots, calm storytelling
Jobstop the scroll in the first secondslet people take in the place

Crop a horizontal edit to vertical and you get a slow video that people scroll past. Stretch a short to a minute and you get a jumpy, restless YouTube video. So the edit is done twice, from the same selected material.

Step 1. Put all the clips in one folder

Don't pre-select the "best" ones — that is where most of the time goes, and you will second-guess it anyway. Drop everything you shot in one place or on one day into one folder. For the Hawaii video that was 30 clips of a few seconds each.

A few practical tips:

  • One location, one folder. A beach video and a city video work better separately.
  • The more different shots, the livelier the result. Five clips of the same sunset make a dull video.
  • Horizontal clips work for both formats: the vertical version uses the centre of the frame.

Step 2. Pick the best moments

This is the slowest part of editing by hand: watch everything, find the good seconds in each clip, drop the shaky, dark and blurry bits.

In NICS Video an automatic editing algorithm does this step. It splits every clip into separate shots, scores sharpness and motion, and keeps the strongest moments. Nothing is invented or generated — only what you filmed ends up in the video.

Step 3. Build the rhythm: opening, middle, ending

A good travel video rests on three things:

  1. A strong opening. The first 1–2 seconds decide whether a short gets watched. Put the most striking shot there, not "here we are getting into the car".
  2. Variety. No more than one or two cuts in a row from the same file, or the video turns into one long shot.
  3. An ending. A sunset, a wide view — a shot that tells the viewer the story is over.

That is how both videos are built in NICS Video: short cuts and quick transitions for the short, long shots and smooth cuts for the horizontal one. If there is not enough footage, the video honestly gets shorter: no shot is repeated to fill time, and you see a warning.

Step 4. Music

Music works best when the video starts on the most energetic part of the track, not on the intro. So the track starts from a strong moment, not from second zero. Music is optional — you can keep the original sound.

Step 5. Covers in two languages

The cover decides whether people click on YouTube and on your profile. We make covers from a real frame of your video with a large headline — separately for the vertical and horizontal video, in English and Russian. A second language helps if you want viewers beyond one audience.

What came out of the Hawaii trip

From 30 clips:

  • a vertical video of about 30 seconds for Shorts, Reels and TikTok;
  • a horizontal video of about a minute and a half for YouTube;
  • 4 covers: one per video in each of the two languages.

What automatic editing does not do

To be clear about the limits:

  • It does not write a script or build a narrated, storyboarded video — that is a job for a human editor.
  • Subtitles are not burned into the video by default.
  • It works best for trips, walks and events, where there are many different shots. Nobody can make a good short out of one long static clip.

In short

To get both Shorts and a YouTube video out of holiday clips: put one location's clips in one folder, pick the good moments, build two different rhythms, start the music on a strong beat and make covers. By hand that is an evening of work. With NICS Video it is a few minutes of waiting for a small folder, and you get finished files ready to publish.
