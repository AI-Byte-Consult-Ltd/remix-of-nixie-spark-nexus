Most forex accounts are not lost on one terrible trade. They are lost on ordinary losing streaks traded with too much size, no stop, or several positions that turn out to be the same bet. Risk management is the set of rules that prevents that. Here are eight that cover most of it.

## 1. Fix the risk per trade in money

Decide what percentage of your account a single trade may lose — usually 0.5–2% — and convert it into money before every trade. At 1% a run of ten losses costs about 9.6% of the account; at 5% it costs about 40%. Details and tables: [how to calculate risk per trade](/blog/how-to-calculate-risk-per-trade/).

## 2. Every trade has a stop loss before it is opened

The stop goes where your idea is proven wrong — beyond a level, outside normal volatility — not at a round number you feel comfortable with. A trade without a stop has unknown risk, and unknown risk cannot be sized.

## 3. Let the stop decide the lot, not the other way round

With the risk amount and the stop distance known, the lot size is arithmetic: risk ÷ (stop in pips × pip value). Round down to your broker's lot step. Full walkthrough: [how to calculate lot size](/blog/how-to-calculate-lot-size-forex/).

## 4. Count correlated positions as one

EUR/USD, GBP/USD and AUD/USD often move together because they all contain the US dollar. Three longs at 1% each can behave like one 3% trade against the dollar. Add up the risk of positions that share a currency or a theme, and cap the total.

## 5. Know the calendar

Interest-rate decisions, inflation data and US jobs reports can move prices more in a minute than in the rest of the day. Spreads widen and stops can be filled worse than planned. Before entering, check whether a high-impact event is due before your trade is likely to finish; with an open position, decide in advance whether to hold through it, reduce it or close it.

## 6. Treat leverage as a limit, not a target

High leverage lets you open large positions with little margin; it does not change how much you should risk. Size by your stop and risk percent, then check that the margin used leaves enough free funds so normal swings do not force a margin call before your stop.

## 7. Set daily and weekly loss limits

For example: stop trading for the day after −3%, for the week after −6%. Limits protect you from the trades you take when you are tired, angry or trying to win back a loss — usually the worst trades of the month.

## 8. Keep a journal in R

Write down every trade: instrument, reason, entry, stop, targets, result in R and what you would do differently. After 50–100 trades you will know your real win rate and [risk-to-reward](/blog/risk-to-reward-ratio/), which is the only honest basis for changing anything.

## A one-minute pre-trade checklist

- Is the stop placed where the idea is invalid?
- Is the risk within my per-trade limit, in money?
- Do I already hold correlated positions?
- Is there a high-impact event before the trade can play out?
- Am I under my daily loss limit?
