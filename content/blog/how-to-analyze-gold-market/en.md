Gold is one of the most traded instruments in the world and one of the most emotional. It reacts to the US dollar, interest rates, fear and central bank buying — sometimes all in the same week. A repeatable routine helps more than any single indicator. Here is one.

## 1. Understand what moves gold

- **The US dollar.** Gold is priced in dollars. A stronger dollar often weighs on gold, a weaker one supports it — a tendency, not a law.
- **Real interest rates.** Gold pays no interest. When inflation-adjusted yields rise, holding gold costs more in missed return, which tends to push it down; falling real yields tend to help it.
- **Fear and uncertainty.** In crises investors buy gold as a safe haven, sometimes regardless of the dollar.
- **Central bank buying.** Large, steady purchases by central banks have supported demand in recent years.

You do not need to forecast these. You need to know which one the market is reacting to *this week*, because it tells you which news can move the price.

## 2. Know the calendar

The biggest gold moves cluster around US data and central bank events: inflation (CPI), the jobs report, Federal Reserve decisions and the Fed chair's press conference. Before any trade, check what is due in the next hours. A good setup two minutes before a rate decision is not a good setup.

## 3. Respect the sessions

Gold trades almost 24 hours on weekdays, but it does not move evenly:

| Session | Typical behaviour |
|---|---|
| Asia | Quieter, often a range |
| London open | Volatility picks up, the Asian range is often tested |
| New York morning | US data and the London–New York overlap bring the biggest moves |
| Late US / rollover | Thin liquidity; spreads can widen sharply around the daily rollover |

## 4. Work top-down on several timeframes

1. **Daily (D1):** direction of the main trend and the major highs and lows. These are where large moves often stall.
2. **4-hour (H4):** the current swing — is price pushing to a new high, or pulling back?
3. **15-minute (M15):** the structure where an entry forms — a pullback into a level, a break and retest.
4. **5-minute (M5):** timing the entry and placing the stop precisely.

Trades in the direction of the higher timeframes need less proof. Trades against them need a clear reason — and usually a smaller target.

## 5. Size the stop for gold's volatility

Gold moves a lot in dollar terms. A stop of a few dollars may sit inside normal noise on a busy day. Use the **ATR** (average true range) on your entry timeframe to see how far price typically travels, and place the stop beyond the level *and* outside that noise. Then calculate the lot from the stop — remember that on most MT5 accounts one lot is 100 ounces, so $1 of price equals $100 per lot. Worked example: [how to calculate lot size for gold](/blog/how-to-calculate-lot-size-forex/).

## 6. Write the plan before you enter

Direction, entry zone, stop, one or more targets, the event that could invalidate it, and the money at risk. If you cannot write it in one line, the setup is not clear yet.

## A daily gold routine in five minutes

- Check the calendar for US data and Fed events today.
- Mark D1 and H4 highs and lows.
- Note whether price is trending or ranging on H4.
- Wait for an M15/M5 setup at a marked level, in the direction of the higher timeframe.
- Size the position from your stop and your [risk per trade](/blog/how-to-calculate-risk-per-trade/).
